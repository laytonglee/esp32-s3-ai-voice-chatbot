How it works

The device listens. The cloud thinks.

A microcontroller can't run a large language model, so Pocket AI Bot splits the job: the always-on listening happens on the chip, and the heavy AI runs on the XiaoZhi server.

Wake

The mic streams audio to the ESP32-S3, where WakeNet checks every 30 ms for the wake word, fully offline.

Listen

The face switches to "listening" and your voice is compressed with Opus and streamed over Wi-Fi.

Think

The server turns speech into text and asks the language model. If needed, the model calls a device tool, such as setting the volume.

Answer

The reply is turned into speech one sentence at a time and played through the speaker, so you hear it before it's even finished.

Typical end-to-end reply time with the default XiaoZhi stack: about 1.84 s (speech recognition ≈ 0.99 s + language model and speech ≈ 0.85 s). It varies with network and model choice.

Does it need the internet?

Yes, to chat. Only the wake word runs offline.

Under the hood

ESP-SR WakeNet

Espressif's on-chip wake-word engine. It turns audio into MFCC features and runs a small CNN using the S3's AI instructions, at about 3 ms of CPU per frame.

Opus streaming

Voice is compressed into 60 ms Opus packets and sent over WebSocket or MQTT + UDP, light enough for a microcontroller and a weak Wi-Fi link.

MCP device tools

The bot exposes actions like volume and display mode through the Model Context Protocol, so the AI can control the hardware itself.

Hardware

Five parts. One tiny board.

An all-digital audio path keeps the build simple: no analog tuning, very few extra components, and everything fits on a breadboard.

🧠
The brain

XIAO ESP32-S3

Dual-core 240 MHz, 8 MB flash + 8 MB PSRAM, Wi-Fi + BLE 5, USB-C and a built-in battery charger. 21 × 17.8 mm.

🎙️
The ears

INMP441

Digital MEMS microphone with I2S output. No ADC needed.

🔊
The voice

MAX98357A

I2S class-D amplifier with a built-in DAC. Drives the speaker directly.

📢
The mouth

Speaker

Small 4–8 Ω, 2–3 W speaker.

🖥️
The face

SSD1306 OLED

0.96" 128 × 64 display over I2C for the face, status and text.

Circuit

Build

Build your own in an afternoon.

You'll need the five parts above, a breadboard, jumper wires, a USB-C data cable and a soldering iron for the XIAO's headers.

  1. Prepare the XIAO

    Solder the headers (no bridges) and snap on the U.FL Wi-Fi antenna. Without it, range is very poor.

  2. Power rails

    Run 3V3 and GND to one rail pair and 5V to a separate rail. Keep 3.3 V and 5 V clearly apart.

  3. Microphone

    VDD→3V3, GND→GND, L/R→GND, WS→D0, SD→D1, SCK→D8. Point it away from the speaker.

  4. Amplifier + speaker

    Vin→5V, GND→GND, DIN→D2, BCLK→D3, LRC→D6. Leave SD and GAIN open, and screw the speaker into the terminal.

  5. OLED, then check

    VCC→3V3, GND→GND, SDA→D4, SCL→D5. Before plugging in USB, check with a multimeter that 5V/3V3 aren't shorted to GND.

  1. Flash the firmware

    Flash XiaoZhi firmware configured for this pin map. To enter bootloader mode: hold BOOT, plug in USB-C, release BOOT.

  2. Connect to Wi-Fi

    On first boot it opens its own hotspot. Join it from your phone and enter your 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi details (5 GHz is not supported).

  3. Activate in the console

    The bot reads out a 6-digit code. Add it at xiaozhi.me, then pick its name, wake word, voice and personality.

  4. Update when needed

    It checks for updates on startup and can update over the air.

  5. Start chatting

    Say the wake word, wait for the listening face, and talk. Say the wake word again to interrupt it.

The firmware is the open-source xiaozhi-esp32 project, built with ESP-IDF v5.4 or newer. Two settings matter on the XIAO ESP32-S3:

  • PSRAM in octal mode. The wake-word engine needs PSRAM. If it's off, the board crashes when listening starts.
  • 8 MB flash. Many XiaoZhi defaults assume 16 MB, so pick a partition layout that fits 8 MB.
  • Use idf.py app-flash while iterating. It skips rewriting the large speech-model partition.

Troubleshooting

It reboots as soon as it starts listening

PSRAM isn't enabled. Turn on SPI RAM in octal mode in the firmware configuration.

The wake word never triggers

Check the mic wiring: WS and SCK swapped, or L/R not tied to GND, are the usual culprits. Then check the GPIO numbers in the firmware.

It wakes up by itself, or while talking

The speaker's sound is reaching the mic. Move them apart, lower the volume, and keep the mic wires away from the speaker wires.

No sound from the speaker

Check DIN/BCLK/LRC, that the amp gets 5 V, and that the speaker is on the amp's screw terminal. SD must not be pulled low.

The OLED stays blank

SDA/SCL swapped, the wrong I2C address (usually 0x3C), or a module with VCC and GND in a different order.

Can't connect, or the audio is choppy

Use a 2.4 GHz network and make sure the U.FL antenna is attached. Also use a data USB cable, not a charge-only one.
